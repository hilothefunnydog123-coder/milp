# Briefing for an agent sending the YNorth library outreach

Paste this whole file to the agent as its instructions. It is written to be
read by an assistant with browser and email access, acting on behalf of
YNorth's maintainer.

---

## 0. Read this before doing anything

**Compose these as drafts and stop. Do not send. Hand each finished draft to
the human for review, and let them press send.**

Three reasons, all practical rather than procedural:

1. **Two of the three branch email addresses below are inferred from a
   pattern, not confirmed.** Sending to a guessed address either bounces or
   lands on the wrong person's desk. Both are worse than waiting an hour.
2. **A first email to a branch manager can only be sent once.** There is no
   version two. These are real public employees who will remember the first
   impression, and the sender is asking them for a favour with no budget
   attached — goodwill is the entire currency here.
3. **Several sentences in the drafts are not true yet** (see §3). Sending
   before they are true turns a good pitch into a false one.

If the human explicitly tells you to send, send. Until then: draft only.

---

## 1. Who the sender is, and what YNorth is

- **YNorth** is a free web tool that turns the maze of getting out of
  homelessness — 211, Coordinated Entry, vital records, waitlists — into one
  ordered, plain-language plan a person can print and walk out holding.
- A patron describes their situation in their own words, by voice or text, in
  English, Spanish, Vietnamese, Chinese, Tagalog and other languages, and gets
  back: what to do now, what's next, which document each step needs, and real
  local resources with working phone numbers.
- **It was built by a student in Milpitas.** That is not a weakness to hide —
  it is the single most persuasive fact in the entire pitch. Lead with it.
- **Library mode** (already built): on a shared computer, nothing is written to
  the machine's persistent storage, the plan is erased on a one-tap button or
  automatically after inactivity, and the AI outbound-calling feature is turned
  off entirely.

## 2. Facts you may state as true

- YNorth is free, requires no account, shows no ads, and sells no data.
- On a library computer, nothing about the patron is stored on the machine or
  on any server; the plan lives only in the browser tab and is erased when the
  session ends, on demand, or after inactivity.
- The AI cannot invent a resource: it draws on a human-verified list plus
  grounded search with citations.
- YNorth is designed to route people **into** MyConnectSV and the County's
  Coordinated Entry system, not around them.
- Accessibility: larger text, high contrast, reduce-motion support, and
  screen-reader compatibility.

## 3. Facts you must NOT state until they are true — check each one

Ask the human to confirm each before any email goes out. If one is still
false, **delete that sentence from the draft** rather than softening it.

| Claim in the drafts | True yet? |
|---|---|
| "fiscally sponsored by The Hack Foundation, a 501(c)(3)" | Only after the HCB application is **approved**, not merely submitted |
| The `ynorth.org/for-libraries` link | Only if that page exists and loads |
| Any real domain (not a `netlify.app` URL) | Only if it resolves |
| "every phone number has been verified" | Only if the human has actually called them |
| WCAG 2.1 AA conformance / "VPAT available" | Only after an audit has been run |

Never invent a statistic, a pilot result, a partner, or an endorsement. If a
recipient asks something you don't know, the answer is "I'll find out and come
back to you" — not a guess.

## 4. Recipients, in priority order

Send **Milpitas first, alone**, and wait for a reply before the other two
branches. If Milpitas says yes, that becomes evidence you can cite in the next
email. Blasting all three at once throws that away.

### Verify addresses before drafting

1. Open **https://sccld.org/community-library-managers/**
2. Read off the manager's **name** for each branch below and use it in the
   greeting. "Dear Manager" reads as a mail merge and gets deleted.
3. Confirm each email address. The district appears to use role inboxes of the
   form `Branch_Manager@lib.sccgov.org`.
4. **If you cannot confirm an address, do not guess it.** Use the branch's
   contact form, or **https://sccld.org/ask-a-librarian/**, and say plainly
   that you're trying to reach the branch manager.

## 6. Rules while sending

- **One branch at a time.** Milpitas → wait → Gilroy and Morgan Hill.
- **Never BCC or mass-send.** Each email is individually addressed and
  individually personalised. A visible mail merge to public librarians is fatal
  to a goodwill-based ask.
- **Plain text.** No tracking pixels, no read receipts, no attachments on a
  first email — attachments from unknown senders get filtered or ignored.
- **One follow-up maximum**, after 7 days, two sentences, then stop. If they
  don't reply, they don't reply; move to the next branch.
- **Send Tuesday–Thursday morning.** Monday is triage and Friday is gone.
- **Log every send** — recipient, date, exact text — so the human can pick up
  the thread without you.

## 7. When someone replies

Do **not** answer substantively on your own. Summarise the reply for the human
and propose a response for them to approve. Specifically:

- **A yes** → hand it to the human immediately. They should reply personally
  and book the meeting themselves; this is the relationship the whole project
  rests on.
- **A question you can answer from §2** → still draft it for approval.
- **A question you cannot** → say so, and flag it. Do not fill the gap.
- **A no** → thank them in one sentence, ask if someone else at the district
  would be a better contact, and move on. Do not argue.
- **Anything about patron data, liability, procurement, or accessibility
  standards** → stop and escalate to the human. Those answers commit the
  project to things an agent should not be committing it to.
